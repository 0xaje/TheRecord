# Nigeria, Africa and public accountability — evidence and product fit

Research date: 18 November 2026. Companion to [Why The Record exists](WHY_THE_RECORD.md). This expands evidence and possible applications, not the implemented product scope. No political promise tracker, government identity system, procurement integration or completion-verification feature has been added.

## The Nigerian starting point

As a Nigerian builder, a defensible motivation is: **people should be able to check what was committed to, who approved that version and what changed later.** Do not claim that Nigeria has no accountability systems, that Nigerians generally lack integrity, or that a blockchain application can force politicians to keep promises.

Distinguish three problems:

| Problem | What evidence is needed | Current product fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disputed or missing terms/approvals | Exact approved document, designated keys and version record | Direct fit when the parties actually use the workflow and keep the original file. |
| A documented commitment is not delivered | Inspection, verified progress, payments and responsibility | Approval record is only a reference. The Record does not establish delivery or enforce it. |
| A fraudulent or unauthorized commitment is approved | Identity, legal authority, procurement checks, investigations | Wallet signature alone does not prove lawful authority or stop collusion. |

## Nigerian evidence

### N1. Tracka: commitments versus physical delivery

BudgIT/Tracka's 2023/2024 annual report describes 1,404 monitored projects across 25 states: 720 completed, 332 ongoing, 223 not executed, and 129 grouped as abandoned/fraudulent/under-delivered. The dashboard gives tracked allocations of approximately N282.51 billion. These are Tracka's monitored projects and classifications, not a random national census or judicial finding that every incomplete project is fraudulent. The report also shows differing coverage of disbursement data. [N1]

**Our inference:** preserve an approved specification and any approved revisions so monitors can compare observed work with a stable reference. Site visits and financial evidence are still essential. Budget allocation is not the same as disbursement, loss or money recoverable by our product. Do not present the N282.51 billion allocation as stolen or preventable loss.

### N2. NOCOPO and OGP: accountability already exists

The Open Contracting Partnership registry identifies the Bureau of Public Procurement's Nigeria Open Contracting Portal, with downloadable procurement data. It also flags data-quality concerns; its quality-review date is May 2023, whereas its retrieval range extends to October 2026. Do not assume old quality findings describe every current record. [N2]

OGP's Nigeria Results Report 2023–2025, published 28 April 2026, records improvements to NOCOPO and standardised procurement access alongside remaining data-entry and paperwork work. It describes both progress and implementation limitations. [N3]

**Our inference:** The Record might complement existing document publication with verifiable approvals and amendment history. It does not replace NOCOPO, prove an agency's data is true, or establish a working integration. Public document access remains necessary; a hash without the document is not meaningful public disclosure.

### N3. Nigeria–P&ID: large disputes are not simply missing signatures

In its 22 October 2025 costs judgment, the UK Supreme Court records Nigeria's successful challenge to the P&ID arbitration awards, exposure exceeding US$11 billion at trial, and unassessed legal costs of £44.217 million excluding interest. The appeal before that court concerned the currency of costs, not a fresh merits trial. [N4]

**Our inference:** trustworthy contracting and evidence handling have serious consequences. This is not evidence that The Record could have prevented P&ID, saved US$11 billion, identified bribery or authenticated officials' authority. False commitments can also be immutably recorded. The app's possible contribution is narrower: preserve a jointly approved version and subsequent approvals, if the right parties use it.

### N4. Public auditors already distinguish promise from implementation

Nigeria's Office of the Auditor-General lists project implementation monitoring, collecting project documents, examining delay/abandonment and checking contract variations among its functions. This establishes an existing oversight mandate, not a measure of its effectiveness or a finding against a particular official. [N5]

**Our inference:** reliable version evidence is one input to an audit; it is not the audit itself.

## Politicians and public promises

Campaign promises, government contracts, budgets and legally authorised payment commitments are different instruments. A campaign statement is not automatically a contract, expenditure approval or guaranteed payment obligation. We must not present wallet approval as changing their legal status.

Existing Nigerian civic trackers, including AdvoKC's Promise Tracker, provide an example of political-promise monitoring. Its indexed project description says researchers assess promises against delivery; the direct page exposed no readable text to this check, so no current politician score/count is relied on here. [N6]

Code for Africa's PromiseTracker methodology describes evidence-based tracking, including historical Nairobi/county-government commitments. It is an existing methodology example, not a claim that the named historical administration remains in office or that every country uses this system. [A1]

A responsible future commitment record would separate:

1. The original statement/document and reliable source.
2. The person or institution making it, with independently established authority.
3. Specific deliverables, location, resources and deadline.
4. Approved changes, preserving the prior version.
5. Progress evidence, inspector/reviewer and disputed findings.
6. A documented explanation of any fulfilled/not-fulfilled rating and a correction process.

The Record currently handles an agreement-version fingerprint and two designated key approvals. It does **not** implement all six layers. A citizen and journalist signing an archived manifesto proves their keys approved that copy, not that the politician signed it or accepted a legal obligation. A politician refusing to participate cannot be made to sign by software. No pledge should be rated “broken” solely because our application has no completion record.

## A second African example: South Africa

In a 3 February 2026 speech discussing 2023–24 municipal audits, South Africa's Auditor-General reported 11 municipalities with disclaimed opinions where evidence was unavailable for most financial-statement information. The speech also described an Inanda housing project: 343 units promised by May 2021, but 29 completed by September 2024. These are dated, scoped audit observations, not a current count of all failures or proof that every non-clean audit involves corruption. [A2]

**Our inference:** a stable record of agreed requirements is useful, but building homes requires funding, procurement, capacity, inspections and enforcement. An approval fingerprint cannot certify a house exists. The Auditor-General expressly emphasises people, leadership and systems working together.

## Applications across everyday life and public institutions

The classifications below are our product-fit analysis, not proof of market demand or features already implemented. Nigeria and other African markets need their own interviews; the earlier UK, Canadian and US cases must not be relabelled as African incidence data.

| Area | Concrete evidence question | Current fit and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freelancers, designers, developers | Was this price, scope and deadline version approved by both keys? | Strongest current fit. No payment guarantee or real-world identity proof. |
| Suppliers and small businesses | Which specification/quantity revision was approved? | Direct document fit; no goods inspection, inventory, invoice matching or funds transfer. |
| Construction and community projects | Was this drawing/specification change approved? | Useful reference; engineering inspection, lawful sign-off and physical completion remain external. |
| Grants, NGOs and community associations | What deliverables/reporting terms were approved? | Possible document use. Does not audit expenditure, identify all stakeholders or prove impact. |
| Schools and training providers | Which service or programme terms were accepted? | Possible non-sensitive agreement use; privacy, authority and special safeguarding requirements need review. |
| Workplace engagements | Which duties or terms were acknowledged? | Records key approvals only; employment rights and informed consent are jurisdiction-specific. |
| Land/property | Which file version was exchanged and approved? | Not a land registry, ownership verification, conveyance or cure for a fraudulent title. Do not market it as one. |
| Public procurement | Which contract/amendment text did designated keys approve? | Future pilot candidate; no procurement compliance, beneficial ownership or authorised-official integration today. |
| Political commitments | Can the original pledge and revisions be checked? | Adjacent research direction, not a delivered promise tracker. Needs source provenance, authority and outcome assessment. |
| Medical/legal/highly sensitive agreements | Which version was approved? | Byte matching is relevant but insufficient. Do not deploy as a compliance-ready specialist product without separate work. |

## How the global evidence fits

The earlier research provides UK uncertainty over contractual terms, Canada's disputed informal acceptance, and US freelancer complaint data. Together with the Nigerian and South African sources, these illustrate distinct evidence and delivery problems in multiple jurisdictions. They are not one global prevalence statistic and do not establish that the same remedy is suitable everywhere. [Global brief](WHY_THE_RECORD.md)

## Recommended direction

Keep today's product focused on two-party agreement-version evidence. Use Nigeria as part of the founder's motivation and a priority place to interview users. Present government/community accountability as a possible application area, not a claim that we have solved public corruption. Any implementation of public pledges, multi-party procurement approval, identity/official-authority binding, inspection evidence or outcome ratings would be a material scope change requiring a separate agreed milestone.

For the next validation round, interview Nigerian freelancers/clients and a community-project monitor separately. Ask which problem they have: unclear terms, lost documents, identity, disputed changes, missing money or undelivered work. Observe the current workaround, the cost and whether exact-version evidence would change a decision. Do not solicit allegations or private documents merely to create a dramatic pitch. Record permission and anonymise notes; no outreach has been sent by the agent.

## Nigeria-rooted founder wording — draft, not posted

I am building The Record as a Nigerian who wants agreements to be easier to check and harder to quietly rewrite. Nigeria already has auditors, civic monitors and open-contracting initiatives, but documented project monitoring shows that recording a commitment and delivering it are still different challenges.

The Record tackles one part: preserving evidence of the exact agreement version approved by two designated wallet keys. It does not force anyone to deliver or prove that an official has acted lawfully. It gives parties a shared reference they can check when terms change or memories differ. I am starting with everyday agreements, while researching how the same evidence principle could complement community and public-project oversight.
